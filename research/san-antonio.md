## Housing Stock
Highly diverse — post-WWII ranches (1950s-70s) in established neighborhoods like Olmos Park, Monte Vista, and the South Side; 1980s-90s brick homes in suburbs like Northside and Northwest; new construction 2000s-present in master-planned areas (Alamo Ranch, Stone Oak, Westover Hills). Slab foundations dominate. Lots range from small urban (4,000-6,000 sq ft) to larger suburban (8,000-15,000 sq ft).

## Systems & Materials
Predominantly Bermuda and St. Augustine grass. Caliche soil throughout — compacts hard, inhibits drainage. Large live oak, cedar elm, and mountain cedar are dominant tree species. Many older neighborhoods have mature pecan and mesquite trees.

## Local Conditions
- Summer heat: 95-100°F average July/August, extreme drought risk
- Water restrictions: Stage 1-3 restrictions common in dry years (SAWS)
- Soil expansion: black clay and caliche swells and cracks
- No real freeze threat for most years, but winter storm events (Uri 2021) cause significant tree damage
- Flash flood risk in low-lying areas

## Common Problems
1. St. Augustine lawn browning and chinch bug damage during summer drought
2. Mountain cedar overgrowth along fence lines and drainage easements
3. Post-storm debris and broken limbs (live oaks are particularly prone)
4. Bermuda grass runners invading beds and flowerbeds
5. Caliche hardpan preventing proper drainage — standing water after rain
6. Overgrown vacant lots adjacent to properties in older neighborhoods
7. HOA violations from unmaintained edges and overgrowth

## Local Texture
Neighborhoods: Stone Oak, Alamo Ranch, Southtown, King William Historic District, Thousand Oaks, Shavano Park, Medical Center area. San Pedro Creek greenway runs through the urban core. Many properties back to greenbelt areas requiring perimeter maintenance.

## Content Angles (San Antonio specific)
1. SAWS water restriction seasons — drought-tolerant lawn care strategies for SA summers
2. Post-freeze storm cleanup — Urban Winter Storm Uri aftermath still visible in some older neighborhoods
3. Live oak management — SA's iconic trees drop leaves in spring (not fall) requiring cleanup timing adjustments
4. Caliche soil — what it means for lawn health and what Elite does differently to work with it
5. HOA lawn compliance — many SA neighborhoods have strict curb appeal rules with violation notices
