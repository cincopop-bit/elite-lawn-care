## Housing Stock
Hill Country ranch-style homes on 1-5 acre lots dominate. Mix of older limestone farmhouses (pre-1990) and newer upscale custom builds (2000s-present). Kendall County's Hill Country Corridor has seen rapid growth. Many properties have long gravel driveways, cedar rail fences, and native landscaping mixed with maintained lawns.

## Local Conditions
- More elevation than SA — slightly cooler, less intense urban heat
- Thin rocky topsoil over limestone karst — tree roots surface visibly
- Heavy cedar and oak pollen seasons drive yard work awareness
- Drought conditions same as SA, but deeper wells mean less SAWS restriction impact
- Flooding risk in Cibolo Creek corridor

## Common Problems
1. Ashe juniper (cedar) encroachment — fast-growing and aggressive on any unmanaged lot
2. Live oak and plateau live oak limb drop — heavy limbs over structures a serious liability
3. Rocky terrain makes mowing difficult without proper equipment
4. Brush pile accumulation on back acreage — fire risk in dry seasons
5. Deer pressure on ornamental plantings — landscaping choices matter here

## Local Texture
Historic downtown Main Street. Kronkopp Park. Cibolo Creek. The Cibolo Nature Center. Local real estate has seen Austin-migration influx post-2020 pushing property values and landscaping expectations up significantly.

## Content Angles (Boerne specific)
1. Cedar clearing — Boerne sits in the heart of cedar country; removal vs. selective clearing decisions matter here
2. Acreage maintenance — multiple-acre lots need different service plans than city lots
3. Wildfire season readiness — brush clearance and defensible space around structures
4. Hill Country rock — mowing around limestone outcroppings without equipment damage
5. Upscale curb appeal — Boerne's growing real estate market means landscaping directly impacts home value
